Managing Exam Anxiety

MindCare Mental Health Library  ·  5 min read

Exam anxiety is one of the most common challenges faced by university students. It's completely normal to feel nervous before an important test — but when anxiety becomes overwhelming, it can actually hurt your performance. The good news? There are proven strategies to manage it.

1. Breathing Techniques

When anxiety strikes, your body enters "fight or flight" mode. Controlled breathing activates your parasympathetic nervous system and calms you down quickly.

Try the 4-7-8 technique: Inhale through your nose for 4 seconds → Hold for 7 seconds → Exhale through your mouth for 8 seconds. Repeat 3 times.

2. Smart Preparation

A huge source of exam anxiety is feeling unprepared. Here's how to study smarter:

  • Start at least 7 days before the exam
  • Break content into small daily chunks
  • Use active recall — test yourself instead of just rereading
  • Review your notes within 24 hours of a lecture
  • Get a study partner or group for accountability

3. Mindset Shifts

How you think about exams matters as much as how you prepare for them.

  • Replace "I must get 100%" with "I will do my best with what I know"
  • One exam does not define your entire future
  • Mistakes are part of learning, not evidence of failure

4. The Night Before

Many students make the mistake of cramming the night before. This actually increases anxiety and reduces memory consolidation.

Instead: Do a light review for 30 minutes, then stop. Get at least 7–8 hours of sleep. Your brain consolidates memory while you sleep.

5. Day of the Exam

  • Eat a proper meal — low blood sugar worsens anxiety
  • Arrive 10–15 minutes early
  • Bring water and any permitted materials
  • Read ALL questions before starting
  • If you freeze, skip and come back

When to Seek Help

If exam anxiety is significantly affecting your daily life, sleep, or appetite for more than a few weeks, please speak with a professional. You don't have to manage this alone.

When to Talk to Someone

MindCare Mental Health Library  ·  4 min read

There's a quiet strength in reaching out. Many of us are taught to push through difficult feelings alone — but talking to someone, whether a friend, counselor, or trusted adult, can make a profound difference.

You Don't Need to Be in Crisis

A common misconception is that you should only seek support when things are very serious. In reality, speaking with a counselor early — when you notice something feels off — is often far more helpful than waiting.

You don't need to have all the words ready. You don't need to fully understand what you're feeling. Showing up is enough.

Signs It May Help to Talk

  • Feeling persistently sad, empty, or hopeless
  • Difficulty concentrating or keeping up with studies
  • Withdrawing from people and activities you used to enjoy
  • Sleeping too much or too little
  • Feeling overwhelmed most of the time
  • Experiencing physical symptoms like headaches or fatigue without clear cause

What a Counseling Session Looks Like

Many students feel nervous before their first session. That's completely understandable. A MindCare counselor will listen without judgment, help you make sense of what you're experiencing, and work with you at your own pace.

Sessions are fully confidential. Nothing you share will be disclosed without your consent, except in rare circumstances involving safety.

How to Get Started

Log in to MindCare and use the Book Counselor page to choose a time that works for you. You can choose an in-person or online session, and leave a brief note if there's something specific on your mind.

Taking the first step is often the hardest part. But you've already started by being here. Book a session when you're ready.
